Help

what each tab shows, how to read the map, and where the numbers stop being reliable (key H)

This cockpit shows how options traders, and the dealers who take the other side of their trades, are positioned in SPX (and SPY, QQQ) through the day. Every page is explained below: what it shows, how to read it, and what each button does. Nothing here is a trade signal — it describes positioning so you can make your own call.

Words used on this page

Call / put
A call gains when price goes up. A put gains when price goes down.
Strike
The price level an option is tied to. The ladders list strikes from high (top) to low (bottom).
0DTE / Week
0DTE = options that expire today. Week (1–7 DTE) = options that expire in the next week.
Premium
The dollars paid for options.
Bought / sold
Whether a trade was a buy (paid the asking price) or a sell (took the bid).
Bullish / bearish flow
Bullish = calls bought or puts sold. Bearish = puts bought or calls sold.
Open interest (OI) / volume
OI = contracts still open from earlier days (counted once each morning). Volume = contracts traded today.
Dealers
Market makers who take the other side of customers’ trades and hedge by buying or selling the index.
Gamma
How fast a dealer’s hedge must change when price moves. Long gamma (blue): dealers buy dips and sell rallies, which calms price. Short gamma (red): dealers sell into drops and buy into rallies, which speeds moves up.
Gamma flip / zero
The price where dealers switch from long to short gamma.
Call wall / put wall (CW / PW)
The strikes with the most call or put positioning. Price often slows or stalls near them.
Pin / pin magnet
A strike price tends to be pulled toward as the close gets near.
Delta
How much an option moves with price. Used here as the size of a directional bet.
Charm
How dealer hedges change just from time passing. Strongest late in the day.
Vanna
How dealer hedges change when implied volatility rises or falls.
Implied volatility (IV) / ATM
IV = how big a move options are priced for. ATM (at the money) = the strike nearest to price.
Straddle
One call plus one put at the money. Its price is the move the market expects.
σ (sigma)
One standard deviation. Price stays inside ±1σ about two times out of three.

How to read it on different days

The same screen means different things on different days. First decide which kind of day it is (the gamma box at the top says long or short gamma; on the Map, Flip line draws the flip as one bold yellow line), then read the pages below in that light. Each page further down has its own “On different days” notes.

Long gamma day
Price is above the flip (blue, above the yellow Flip line). Dealers trade against the move, so ranges hold and moves fade. Map → Rule read: the ceiling is the vanna flip above price, the floor is the gamma flip below; the gamma peak is the pin. Density: narrow cone, the pin band matters. Fade the edges, don’t chase.
Short gamma day
Price is below the flip (red). Dealers trade with the move, so moves stretch and levels break more easily. Rule read swaps the roles: the gamma flip above caps the top, the vanna flip below holds the bottom. Density: a wider cone; the full-book trough is the floor to watch. Don’t fade a break; wait for price to show it is holding.
At the flip
Price inside the dark band between blue and red (the yellow Flip line is its middle): dealers barely hedge either way, so the day’s character is being decided. Chop inside the band is normal; a 5-minute close out of it sets the regime for the next stretch.
Pin into the close
Long gamma after about 2:30 pm. Price tends to settle on the gamma zero line or the pin of the Map’s last smooth column (about 3:45–3:50, not the 4:00 edge). Charm decides the drift from about 3:15 to 3:45; in the last 10 minutes closing orders take over.
Trend day
The flip and the pin keep moving with price, and the range passes about 1.5× the morning straddle (Gap strip on the Map, Straddle page). The levels are moving targets: follow the direction of travel instead of fading fixed levels. On 11/11 the “calm market” read failed this way once the range reached 2.5× the AM straddle.
Vol spike
VIX jumping, VIX9D above VIX. Vanna takes over: when implied vol rises, dealer hedges change with no price move at all. Surface → Gamma + vanna (β): where its sign differs from gamma alone, a vol move can break that level. VIX page → “Why VIX moved”: a whole-curve shift is real repricing; sticky strike is mechanical and tends to come back out.
Gap open
Price opens far from yesterday’s close, before the map has any of today’s trades. The overnight straddle edges (violet lines, Gap strip) and the morning plan’s levels are the first-hour levels: on 11/11 the overnight edge 7,572 and the morning 7,575 caught the open low.

Flow

The main page: which way today’s option money is leaning, strike by strike and minute by minute.

Read line
One sentence: today’s lean, and whether price is above or below the gamma switch (above = hedging calms moves, below = hedging speeds them up).
Protection card
The biggest put bought below price today.
Upside bet card
The biggest call bought above price today.
Crash insurance card
Net buying or selling of far-out puts (under 15 delta). Tail bought = people paying for crash protection. Tail sold = protection being sold. On a falling day a yellow note warns that selling far puts is often cashing in old hedges, not a bullish bet.
Premium slider
Share of today’s bought-or-sold option dollars that were bullish. The small tick = the last logged session.
Delta slider
The same trades weighted by how much market exposure each one adds. Closer to the real directional bet. If the two sliders disagree, trust Delta for direction — expensive puts sold to close can tilt Premium. If both read balanced on a big move, the options tape was two-sided and the move is coming from somewhere else (futures, stocks).
Net delta line
The sum of all customers’ directional exposure from today’s trades, in index-share units. Negative = customers net short the market.
Mood by expiration
(Pulse open) Share of directional premium that was bullish, for each expiry.

Flow bars
Off to start (your own choice is remembered). Colours each minute of the price line by that minute’s near-the-money flow: green = bullish, red = bearish, grey = mixed. W / M arrows mark a double bottom / double top that broke out and where the flow flipped the same way.
Side: Quote / Trade tag
How each trade is called a buy or a sell. Quote = the minute’s last price compared with the bid and ask. Trade tag = each trade’s own buy/sell tag. In Quote, a trade that printed outside that minute’s bid/ask (the quote moved after it, common in fast markets) is left unsigned rather than guessed. Changes the bubbles, flow bars, sliders, volume ladders and the Premium tab’s Through the day charts. Also switchable on the Premium tab (same setting). Per trade = every trade judged on its own: at the ask = buy, at the bid = sell (against its own quote at that moment); anything in between decided by the tick rule (the direction of that contract’s last price change). Spread legs and trades too small for the tape stay unsigned. The footer says how much was decided each way.
Seq
Sequence bubbles. Small grey = after a big move, calls sold then puts bought at a top (or the reverse at a bottom). Big coloured “Reversal” = the same at a key level, with dealers short gamma and the flow flipping. Each is scored 30 minutes later: ✓ worked, ✗ failed.

On different days

Long gamma
Heavy one-way flow near a wall tends to get absorbed: if the bubbles stay one colour but price stalls, dealers are taking the other side.
Short gamma
Flow and price moving the same way can feed on itself, because dealers hedge in the same direction: don’t fade it.
Big move, sliders balanced
If price moves a lot while Premium and Delta read balanced, the move is coming from futures or stocks, not options.
Falling day
Far puts sold is often old hedges being cashed in (the yellow note), not a bullish bet.

Orbs

Dealer gamma at every strike through the day, drawn as orbs, with the volume profile beside it. Off by default; press ON (nothing is fetched or drawn while it is off). Follows the SPX / SPY / QQQ buttons. A positioning read, not a trade signal.

Orbs
Each orb is one strike in one time block. Bigger orb = more dealer hedging tied to that strike. Yellow = positive (dealers trade against the move there, so price tends to slow, stall or pin). Purple = negative (dealers trade with the move, so price can speed up through it). Candles = price in the same blocks. Orb size is scaled to the biggest value so far, so a replay never uses a later, bigger number.
Show
Gamma = dollars dealers must buy or sell for each 1% move. Vanna = how those hedges change when volatility moves 1 point. Charm = how much dealers’ delta changes per hour from time passing alone, in dollars of the index (yellow = their delta grows, so they sell to stay hedged; purple = it shrinks, so they buy), worked out with each strike’s own implied volatility and real calendar time to the expiry. Both = gamma filled, vanna as rings. All of them follow Positions from.
Positions from
The same four choices as the Map, one at a time. OI = contracts open at the start of the day. Vol = only contracts traded today. OI + vol = both, with the same simple rule (dealers long calls, short puts). OI + signed = open interest plus today’s trades by their buy/sell tag: a customer buy leaves the dealer short that option, a customer sell leaves the dealer long.
Price ±1% / ±2% / All, Reset
How much price the charts show around the current price (±2% to start). The mouse wheel on any of the three charts zooms in or out around the pointer, dragging up or down moves the range, and a double-click or Reset goes back to the button’s range. The orbs, the gamma ladder and the volume profile always show the same prices.
Expiries
Today = same-day options only. Next 7 days = tomorrow through 7 days out. All = both. About ±200 SPX points (±2.7% for SPY / QQQ).
Volume
Contracts traded today at each strike, up to the time on the slider, drawn either side of a centre line. Calls/Puts: calls to the right, puts to the left; solid = bought, striped = sold, faint = no buy/sell tag. Bull/Bear: bull to the right = calls bought (solid) + puts sold (striped); bear to the left = puts bought (solid) + calls sold (striped). Net = Bull minus Bear: green bar to the right when more bullish, red to the left when more bearish. Buy / sell comes from each trade's own tag. POC (orange line) = the busiest strike for the totals shown (Calls/Puts: calls + puts; Bull/Bear: bull + bear; Net: the biggest net either way); dashed VAH / VAL = the strikes that hold 70% of it. The orange dotted line on the orbs = where the POC sat through the day.
Block
1, 3 or 5 minutes per orb column and candle (1 minute is the finest: a new reading arrives once a minute). A block that is still filling has a yellow dashed frame marked building.
Play / slider / Now
Play replays the day; nothing to the right of the slider is shown, so it never peeks ahead. Hover an orb or a volume bar for its numbers; click the orbs to jump there. Now = back to the latest minute.
How fresh
The options feed is read once a minute. A new minute shows up here a few seconds after it is read (the tab checks every 3 seconds). The stand-alone page flow_orbs.html shows the same data.

On different days

Falling day
Put skew rising with the drop = real demand for protection. Skew turning toward calls while price makes new lows = sellers running out: in our 2019–2026 test that tell was followed by bounces (ETFs +4.1% over 10 days on average vs +1.3% normally), but it failed in true bear legs such as September 2022.

Which options the map uses: today’s, the next week’s, or both (key E). Check the read-out: often most of the gamma at price is in the Week book, so 0DTE alone can mislead.
OI / Vol / OI + vol / OI + signed

Rule read
Button above the read-out, off by default. Picks the ceiling and the floor by the gamma regime: in long gamma the vanna flip caps the top and the gamma flip floors the bottom; in short gamma the gamma flip caps the top and the vanna flip floors the bottom. Each level says how many of the other levels sit within about 0.1% of it (the other greek’s flip, the call or put wall, the AM levels). The gamma peak is called the pin: price tends to sit there, but it moves with price, so it is not a ceiling. One charm line; the into-the-close pin line shows only in long gamma from 3:15 to 3:45. Vanna and charm use the right map’s book. Off = the read-out as before.
Flip line
Button above the gamma map, off by default. Draws the flip as one bold yellow line (dark edge) on the normal map: where gamma changes sign, taking the crossing nearest price at each minute. The map’s own colours stay as they are, so the dark band between blue and red is the transition zone and the yellow line is its middle. The label gives the flip and the zone at the current minute (zone = where gamma is under 10% of that minute’s strongest value). The thin yellow line is still the trough; the flip line is the thick one.
Zone
Button above the gamma map, off by default. Redraws the gamma map in two colours from the same numbers: green = dealers long gamma (they calm moves), red = short gamma (they speed moves up). The bold yellow line is the flip: where gamma changes sign, taking the crossing nearest price at each minute. The light band around it is the transition zone: prices where dealer gamma is weak (under 10% of that minute’s strongest value), so hedging neither calms nor speeds price. The label gives the flip and the zone at the current minute. A narrow band = a sharp switch; a wide band = a soft one where price can chop. Zone ignores De-trend, so the colours always mean long or short.
Map read-out
The panel under “Breakdown by strike”: a plain bottom line first (calm or fast market, ceiling, flip line, downside magnet, what a vol rise or the clock does, where the gamma sits, outside levels that agree), then the Week book check, then Details with every number. Details stays open or closed as you left it.
Ledger
Test view. Starts today’s book from a running count of customers’ buys minus sells from earlier days, then adds today’s trades. The closest estimate of real dealer positions, but scored nightly before it is trusted.
De-trend
Removes each minute’s background level so small structure shows. Warning: blue and red then mean “above or below this minute’s average”, not long or short.
AM cone
Amber cone from the open, sized by this morning’s 0DTE IV: ±1σ dashed, ±2σ dotted. Frozen for the day.
Live cone
Thin white cone that follows price and live IV.
Speed
Pink dotted lines where gamma changes fastest with price: the edges where a pin starts to let go. Test view.
Smile
Lets each strike’s IV move with price when computing gamma and charm, by the amount actually measured: re-measured every night from our own data (same-day options in 15-minute steps, longer expiries day to day). In practice IV at a fixed strike falls as SPX rises and rises as SPX falls. Test view, scored nightly against the plain map.
Var clock
Lets the projection spend time like the market does (busy open and close, quiet lunch). Test view.
Gap
Strip above the map comparing the opening gap with the overnight straddle, plus violet lines at prior close ± the overnight straddle. Says how much of the priced move the day has used.
Breakdown by strike
Middle panel: dealer position at each strike (puts left, calls right). The dot = start of the day, so dot-to-bar = what today changed. The text below reads the map in plain words.
Premium / AM
Draw the Premium page’s lines, or the morning levels, on the maps.
Charm / Vanna / Change
Right panel. Charm = dealer buying or selling from time passing. Vanna = what dealers do if volatility rises one point. Change = where gamma was added (orange) or removed (cyan) in the last 30 minutes; needs the Vol or OI + vol basis.
0DTE / Week / All (right)
The right map has its own book choice, so you can read gamma on one book and vanna on another.
Zoom, ±25 / 50 / 100, 9:30 → / Last 2h →
Zoom all three maps to a price window around spot and a time window.
Classic / Gradient
Colour style. Gradient gives each size its own colour.
Day / Column
Colour scale. Day = one scale for the whole map (mornings can look dim). Column = each minute scaled on its own, so the shape shows all day.
Why vanna can look flat on All
On the All book a large constant from the Week book is removed from vanna and charm automatically. On 0DTE and Week they are left as they are.

On different days

Long gamma

Gamma
Dollars of the index dealers must buy or sell for a 1% move. Positive (ridge) = dealers long gamma: they sell rallies and buy dips, so price slows or sticks. Negative (valley) = short gamma: they chase, so moves speed up. Net gamma changes sign once near price: that edge is the flip.

Dollars of delta dealers gain for a 1-point rise in implied vol. Positive: a vol rise makes them sell and a vol drop makes them buy (vol falling into a rally supports it). Negative: the reverse. Charm
Dollars of delta dealers gain each hour from time passing alone. Positive = they sell each hour, negative = they buy. It sits almost all in today’s expiry and is strongest late in the day; read it from about 3:15 to 3:45.

Mountains, valleys and cliffs

Gamma
Mountain = dealers long gamma at those strikes: they sell rallies and buy dips, so price slows, sticks or pins there. Valley = short gamma: they chase, so price runs through fast. Cliff (mountain dropping into valley) = the flip: crossing it switches the day from calm to fast. A steep cliff = a sharp switch; a gentle slope = a choppy one. Front to back: a mountain only in today’s row is gone at 4 pm.
Vanna
Mountain = when vol rises dealers sell, when it falls they buy (a rally with vol falling gets their buying). Valley = the reverse. Cliff = a vanna flip. There are many; only the one next to price counts: in long gamma the cliff just above price tends to cap the high, in short gamma the one just below tends to hold the low.
Charm
Mountain = time passing alone makes dealers sell at those strikes each hour (a drift down). Valley = time makes them buy (a drift up). Cliff = usually right at price; whichever side is bigger sets the late-day drift. Matters most from 3:15 to 3:45.
Gamma + vanna (β)
Read like gamma, but after vol moves with price. A spot that is a mountain on gamma and a valley here = a calm spot a vol spike can break. A cliff that sits away from gamma’s cliff = the flip moves when vol moves.
Shape
Mountain = gamma and vanna both positive: the strongest stall or pin. Valley = both negative: the strongest speed-up. Cliff = both switch together: the strongest transition.
Transition zone
On the Surface it is the gamma cliff nearest price. On the Map it is the dark band between blue and red; the Flip line marks its middle.

Long gamma
Price sits on the ridge. The vanna colour change just above price is the likely cap for the high.
Short gamma
Price sits in a valley. The vanna colour change just below price is the likely floor; expect bigger swings.
Vol spike
Switch to Gamma + vanna (β): where it disagrees with plain gamma, a pin or floor can give way when implied vol jumps.
Into the close
Charm · Today: the sign around price says which way time alone pushes dealers each hour; it fades in the last 10 minutes.
Trend day
With OI + vol the ridge moves with price as new trades come in. Re-check every half hour; a ridge left far behind no longer matters.

Levels

Price levels where several different ways of finding a level point at the same place, rebuilt every evening after the close. Off by default; press ON. A positioning read, not a trade signal: it shows where the views agree, not which way price will go or whether a level will hold.

The five views
Greek peaks: where the option book is most sensitive (largest vomma with open interest, where dealer gamma bends hardest, where dealer gamma crosses zero). Open interest: strikes with the most open put and call contracts, up to 45 days. VIX options: the biggest VIX option strikes turned into SPX levels (about 2 VIX points per 1% SPX move; the recently measured figure is shown too). Price memory: 20/50/100/200-day averages, recent swing highs and lows, last month’s high and low, volume-weighted average price from a swing. Crowded strikes: the 22- and 25-delta strikes of the next weekly and monthly expiries, and strikes of the largest index block trades.
Bands
A band is a zone where levels from different views fall within 25 SPX points of each other. Yellow, bold = 3 or more views agree (a key zone). Grey, faint = 2 views. Thin coloured ticks on the right edge = a level from one view only. The coloured dots in a band show which views agree.
Vol-shock peak
The hollow dashed ring. The strike where a single option’s vega reacts most to a jump in volatility, at today’s price, vol and time. It does not depend on anyone’s position, so it is shown for reference and never counts toward a band.
Yesterday
A white hollow diamond with an arrow shows where a zone sat the previous evening and how far it moved.
Price ±1% / ±2% / All, Reset
How much price the chart shows around spot (All to start, the whole chart). The mouse wheel on the chart zooms around the pointer, dragging up or down moves the range, and a double-click or Reset goes back to the button’s range. Hover cards and taps still work when zoomed.
Hover / tap
Point at (or tap) a band for a card with its middle and range, the distance from spot, each view and its exact number, what changed since the previous evening, and the track record so far. The chips under the chart pick out each key zone; tap one to light up its band.
Track record
Each stored zone is checked over the next 5 sessions: did SPX trade into it, and did it close back on the side it came from (held) or go through (broke). Key zones are compared with single-view levels. Under 30 cases the numbers mean very little.
Limits
Built from the end-of-day option book and daily prices. Dealer sides are assumed (calls +, puts −), not measured. Levels further than 8% from spot are not drawn.

What to trust, and when

  1. Delta beats premium for direction. On a falling day, puts sold to close (people cashing in protection) count as “bullish” dollars and can tilt the Premium slider. The Delta slider weights each trade by its market exposure, so it is less affected. If both read balanced on a big move, the options tape was two-sided and the move is coming from elsewhere.
  2. Side: Quote in fast markets. When option prices jump within a minute, a trade can print outside the new quote. Those trades are left unsigned, so on wild days more of the volume is grey. Compare with Trade tag.
  3. A wall on the edge of the ladder is not a wall. The 0DTE chain covers about ±100 points. A wall on the very first or last row is where the data stops. A wall with rows on both sides is real.
  4. 0DTE alone can be a small part of the picture. Check the Map read-out: when most gamma at price sits in the Week book, read the All book.
  5. Gamma above the Week book’s top strike can show the wrong sign. The Week book is built once in the morning over a fixed range. On a big trend day price can run past it.
  6. Vanna can hide disagreements that gamma shows. Vanna has its background removed, so two different books can look alike on vanna. Gamma shows the difference.
  7. Test views are not trusted yet. OI + signed, Ledger, Speed, Smile and Var clock are scored every night against the standard view before they are relied on.
  8. Different pages, different flips. The Map re-prices the 0DTE + 1–7 DTE book every minute (today 7,601); the top bar, Density (front 7,603, full 7,597), Levels (last night’s book) and the AI tools each use their own book and method. Within about 10 points they agree; further apart, the books disagree — read the Map’s.
  9. The dealer side is assumed. Most views use the simple rule (dealers long calls, short puts). Where the signs come out right, the size is still far too big: our hedge table ran about 30× VolSignals’ signed one on Nov 10, mostly because every monthly and quarterly put counts as dealer-short. Read where, not how much.
  10. Screenshots on this page are real screens from Thu Nov 12 at 12:06 ET (long gamma above the flip). On another day the same page looks different; the “On different days” notes say how to read it then.
  11. Basis is shared. The OI / Vol setting on Flow and Map is the same switch. If two browser tabs disagree, refresh the older one.
  12. Regular hours only. Premium and Flow leave out pre-open trading and anything after the close.
  13. The Change view cannot work on OI. OI is one number for the whole day, so it needs the Vol or OI + vol basis.
  14. A sudden lone spike after a flat stretch can be late-arriving volume landing in the wrong minute. Worth a second look.
