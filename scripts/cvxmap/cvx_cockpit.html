The tabs

Flow
The order-flow lean. Signed premium split bought vs sold, the bullish/bearish needle, the biggest protection and upside trades, and a marker for the last logged session.
Strikes
Per-strike detail for whichever strike you have selected. Use the up and down arrows to walk through them.
Skew
The shape of implied volatility across strikes, and how it has moved since the open.
Map
Gamma, charm and vanna as heat maps. Left of the dashed vertical is the book as it actually stood each minute. Right of it is today’s book carried forward to the close as time decays.
Volume
Three ladders side by side: everything so far today, just the last 30 minutes, and the last 30 compared with the 30 before it.
Premium
Where today’s option money actually went — the heaviest strikes a side, how much of each was bought rather than sold, and which minutes carried outsized premium.
Plan
Doc’s morning plan graded against what price has since done, plus the morning book.

Reading the map

Blue / red field
Blue means dealers are long gamma there and absorb moves. Red means they are short and chase them.
Green line
The gamma peak — where dealer gamma is most positive. Reliable even when the book is clipped.
Yellow line
The gamma trough — most negative. Where chasing is strongest.
Pale dotted line
The gamma flip, where it crosses zero. This is the one a missing strike range can move, so treat it with care near the top and bottom of the map.
Thin white curves
Plus and minus one standard deviation, anchored at 9:35.
Solid white line
The spot path. Before the open it is dashed, because SPX does not print then and the level is implied from SPY.
Black dashed vertical
Now. History to the left, projection to the right.
Red dashed horizontal
Current spot.
Green and yellow lines
Peak and trough. They now appear on vanna and charm as well as gamma, so the dotted zero line is no longer the only feature on those maps.
De-trend button
Takes the middle value of each column and subtracts it from every cell in that column, so the column re-centres on zero. Small structure that was buried under a big constant becomes visible — but the sign stops meaning what it meant: blue and red become “above and below the middle of this minute” rather than long and short. Like measuring waves against the water level instead of the sea floor. The caption says so while it is on. Off by default, and with it off every map draws exactly as it always has.
Why vanna already looks de-trended on All
Because it is. On the All book the week book adds a large constant that would bury everything, so vanna and charm have that removed automatically. On 0DTE and Week there is no such constant, so they are left raw and the sign is real.
Change view
On the right-hand map, next to Charm and Vanna. Shows how gamma has moved over the last 30 minutes — orange where it was added, cyan where it was removed. Needs the Vol or OI + vol basis, and says so if you are on OI.

What to trust, and when

  1. A wall sitting on the edge of the ladder is not a wall. The 0DTE chain only covers about ±100 points around spot. If the put wall or call wall lands on the very first or last row, that is where your data stops, not where the wall is. A wall with rows either side of it is real.
  2. Gamma above the week book’s top strike can show the wrong sign. The 1–7 DTE book is built once in the morning across a fixed strike range and does not follow price. On a trending day price can run up near the top of it, and above the last strike there is nothing left but a fading tail. On 26 Oct that tail read dealers as short gamma above spot when the full book had them clearly long. Check how far the week book reaches before trusting gamma near the top.
  3. For gamma, Convex is usually the safer week source. Open interest settles overnight and does not move during the day, so the 08:35 snapshot costs little — and it covers far more strikes. For vanna, TOS live has the better volatility but a much narrower range of strikes.
  4. Vanna hides disagreements that gamma shows. The vanna map has its background level removed so structure stands out. That also means two very different week books can look almost the same on vanna while the numbers underneath differ by a third. Gamma is not treated that way, so it shows the disagreement honestly.
  5. Basis is set on the Flow tab and governs the maps. OI, today’s volume, or both. It is also shown and switchable in the Gamma pane header. If two browser tabs disagree, one of them loaded before you last changed it — refresh.
  6. Premium and Flow count regular hours only. Pre-open trading and anything after the 16:15 close are left out, so the lean reflects the session rather than the roll.
  7. The change view cannot work on the OI basis. Open interest is a once-a-day figure — it settles overnight and the same number is used for every minute of the session. Differencing it would show gamma drifting from price, volatility and time decay, not positioning being built. On the Vol and OI + vol bases today’s volume is in the book and the difference is real, so those are the only two it will draw.
  8. A quiet minute is not always a quiet minute. If the workbook ever falls behind, a strike’s volume can arrive late and land in the wrong minute. Large isolated spikes after a flat stretch are worth a second look.
